Home · Privacy

No cloud. No account. Nothing to leak.

Aura has nowhere to send your listening — there is no server on the other end. Your files, tags, history and recommendations never leave the device they were made on.

What stays on your phone

  • Downloaded audio and saved artwork
  • Tags, filters, sorts and Saved songs
  • Play history and what Aura learns about your taste
  • Queue state and backup files (kept wherever you put them)

What the app accesses, and why

AccessWhy
InternetSong lookups, audio downloads, artwork. Nothing else.
Foreground media playbackBackground audio with notification + lock-screen controls.
Notifications (Android 13+)Playback controls only. Asked at runtime; declinable.

There are no advertising, tracking or crash-reporting tools built in. No storage permission is needed — audio lives in its own storage space on your phone. Aura never asks for contacts, location, or an advertising ID.

What leaves your phone

Only the network requests you trigger: searching, previewing, downloading, and loading artwork. Backups leave the phone only when you share or move the export file. There is no account system, so there is nothing to “delete your data” from — uninstalling removes the on-device library.

Open source verification

Aura is MIT-licensed and open source, developed by Ai-Chetan for the open-source community. You can read the source, build it yourself, and confirm there is nothing to phone home to. Contributors are welcome — see the contributing guide. Questions? Open an issue on GitHub.

Music rights

Aura is a private player, not a music provider: it does not own, host, or license any songs. All music, artwork, and trademarks belong to their respective artists, labels, and rights holders. The app only keeps what you choose on your own device for offline listening, with no sharing or redistribution — please keep only content you own or have permission to store, and respect creators and platform terms.
