How to organize your music with tags instead of playlists

September 2026 · 5 min read

A song can be gym and 90s and acoustic at once — but a playlist forces it into one box. Tags don't.

The playlist problem

Playlists are single-membership: “Workout Mix” duplicates “90s Hits” duplicates “Acoustic Morning”, and every copy drifts out of sync. At a few hundred tracks you stop trusting your own library — not because the music is bad, but because the filing system lies.

Tags are many-to-many

A tag system lets one track carry unlimited labels. In Aura, a song can be gym + 90s + remaster simultaneously, and:

  • AND narrows: gym AND 90s — only tracks with both.
  • OR widens: gym OR run — everything for moving.
  • Search stacks: filter by tags, then search title/artist inside the result.
  • Sorts persist: six orders — recent, oldest, title A–Z/Z–A, longest/shortest.

A starter system that works

  1. Energy first: focus, gym, sleep, drive, party — how you actually pick music.
  2. Then texture: acoustic, live, instrumental, remaster.
  3. Cap it at ~12 tags. More tags than that and filtering gets slower than scrolling.
  4. Tag on save. Two seconds at download time beats a weekend of re-filing.

Why this matters offline

Offline libraries can't lean on search-as-a-service — there is no cloud index to query. Local tags, local filters and local sort are what keep a 2,000-track offline vault navigable in airplane mode. Aura's shuffle play even respects your active filters, so gym AND 90s shuffles exactly that.
