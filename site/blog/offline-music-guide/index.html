How to listen to music offline on Android

September 2026 · 5 min read

Flights, tunnels, data-saver days, dead zones. A ten-minute setup is the difference between silence and your whole library.

1. Know the two kinds of “saved”

Most apps blur this, so let's be explicit. In Aura there are downloads — files on your phone that play with zero signal — and Saved songs — streaming shortcuts that start near-instantly but need a connection. For a flight, only downloads count. Check which is which before you leave Wi-Fi.

2. Download on Wi-Fi, in the background

Queue what you need before you need it: Aura imports playlists of up to 50 tracks in the background, so you can start a batch and put the phone down. The queue shows overall and per-song progress, lets you cancel anytime, and tells you plainly if anything failed.

3. Check storage without granting everything

Aura uses its own storage space on your phone — it never asks for access to all your files. A thousand tracks in original quality is roughly a few gigabytes; check Settings → Storage if a batch stalls, and clear saved artwork first (it downloads again by itself).

4. Set up controls before you lock the phone

Offline is when controls matter most — no pulling the phone out on a run. Aura gives you notification and lock-screen controls, a swipeable mini player, and a real queue with shuffle and repeat. On Android 13+, allow notifications when asked, or controls go quiet.

5. Organize so airplane mode stays fun

No cloud search to rescue you at 35,000 feet. Tag songs as you save them (flight, gym, sleep), so filters and shuffle still surface the right thing offline. The method: tags instead of playlists.

6. Back up before you switch phones

Save a backup file — your full library or just selected songs — and keep the file somewhere safe. On the new phone, bring it back with your tags included. Offline libraries are precious precisely because no cloud holds a copy.
