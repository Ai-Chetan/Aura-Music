Home · Offline music player

An offline music player for Android that keeps working with no signal.

Aura stores the songs you save directly on your phone — your files, your tags, your history. Flights, tunnels, data-saver days: the music keeps playing.

What “offline” means in Aura

Downloaded songs live in their own space on your device and play with no mobile signal or Wi-Fi. Your tags, filters, queue and personal radio all work offline too — there is no server to phone home to, because there is no account and no cloud library.

  • No account, no subscription gate on offline playback
  • No ads, ever
  • Continue listening, queues, shuffle and repeat — all offline
  • Lock-screen and notification controls with no signal

Saving music for offline listening

Paste a link — a single track, a Short, or a playlist of up to 50 — and Aura keeps downloading it in the background, even if you leave the screen. Every song is kept in its original quality, any problem track is flagged on its own without stopping the rest, and the download queue shows overall and per-song progress.

Good to know: Saved songs are streaming shortcuts — they need a connection. Anything you download plays fully offline. Aura marks the difference clearly in the library.

Organized for offline life

Offline libraries go stale without organization. Aura's tag system lets one song live under many tags, with mix-and-match filters and six sort orders — so a 2,000-track offline vault stays navigable. Simple backup files mean your offline collection survives a phone change.

Light on storage and battery

Songs are stored once on your phone, and playback is built for the background, so it uses very little battery. Aura uses its own storage space on your phone, so it never asks for access to all your files.

Your music, their rights

Aura doesn't own any songs and claims no rights over them — all music and artwork belong to their respective artists, labels, and rights holders. It simply plays what you choose to keep for private offline listening.
