Aura FAQ

Plain answers about offline playback, Android support, tags, controls, backups and privacy. New here? Start with features or download.

Offline & Android

Is Aura an offline music player?

Yes. Songs you download are stored on your phone and play with no signal and no sign-in. Queues, tags, filters and lock-screen controls all work offline. Read more: offline music player.

Is Aura available for Android?

Yes — Android 8 and newer, as a free app file (APK) from GitHub Releases. Details: Android player.

What's the difference between downloads and Saved tracks?

Downloads live on your phone and play fully offline. Saved tracks are streaming shortcuts — they need a connection but still start near-instantly, with the same tags and filters.

Library & playback

How does Aura organize music with tags?

Each song can carry many tags. Mix and match them to filter, search by title or artist, and sort six ways. Guide: tags instead of playlists.

Does Aura support lock-screen controls and queues?

Yes. Notification + lock-screen controls, a swipeable mini player, and a real queue with play-next, reorder, shuffle and repeat.

What is Spice up and radio?

Aura learns what you like from what you play, replay and skip — privately, on your phone. Spice up keeps recommended picks flowing behind any playlist; the music never stops — every song lines up the next ones.

How do backups work?

Save a backup file — your full library or just selected songs — share it anywhere, and bring everything back, tags included, when you switch phones.

Privacy, price & source

Does Aura have ads, accounts or tracking?

No — no accounts, no ads, no tracking of any kind. See the privacy notes.

Is Aura free and open source?

Yes. Completely free forever — no paywalls, no premium tier. Also MIT-licensed and open source: read the source, report issues, or fork it. Contributors are welcome — see the contributing guide.

Is saving audio allowed?

Aura is a private, on-device player — not a music provider. It doesn't own any songs and claims no rights over them: all music, artwork, and names belong to their respective artists, labels, and rights holders. Aura only keeps what you choose on your own phone for offline listening, with no sharing or redistribution built in. Please save only content you own or have permission to keep, and respect creators and platform terms.

Who owns the music in Aura?

No songs belong to Aura. Every track, artwork, and trademark stays with its respective owner — artists, labels, and platforms such as YouTube™ (Google LLC). Aura hosts nothing and licenses nothing; it simply plays your private collection.

Who made Aura?

Aura is developed by Ai-Chetan for the open-source community (MIT licence). Contributions are welcome — see the contributing guide.

Ready to try it?

Free APK, no account, your music kept local. Aura is open source under MIT — contributors welcome.
